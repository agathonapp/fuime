The honest version

What you are agreeing to.

Read this part twice. It is the part with your name on it.

You are the account owner

Stripe lets a guardian own a connected account on behalf of someone 13 or older. You complete the signup, so the account is legally yours and your kid operates it. That split is the only reason any of this works before they turn 18.

Stripe holds your ID, not fuime

The identity check is Stripe's. The government ID you upload goes to Stripe, who is the party required to run it. fuime never receives those documents and has nowhere to keep them.

You carry the account owner's risk

Chargebacks, refunds, a negative balance, a client who disputes a payment: Stripe comes to the account owner, and that is you. It works the way it would if you opened a Stripe account for yourself, because that is what you are doing.

The fees are billed to you

7% of what gets collected. No monthly fee until the business clears $250 in 30 days, then $15 a month, billed to the account owner. Stripe's card processing is separate and goes to Stripe.

Every invoice, every fee, and every expense they log, totalled into income, expenses and net for the year. The IRS expects a return once net self-employment income passes $400, so fuime flags that line the month it is crossed instead of the following spring.

Questions

The ones parents actually ask.

Is fuime a bank?

No. fuime is software. It is not a bank, it is not a payment processor, and it is not insured by anyone, because it never holds a balance that could be insured. Stripe moves the money and Stripe is the regulated party.

Where does the money actually sit?

In the Stripe account you own, from the moment a client pays until Stripe pays out to the bank account you connected. fuime has no wallet and cannot move funds anywhere, beyond taking its 7% at the point of payment.

What if my kid loses interest and stops using it?

Then it stops costing you anything. The 7% only applies to money collected, and the $15 a month only applies while the business is clearing $250 in 30 days. A quiet account bills nothing, and you can close it whenever you like.

Can I see everything, or just a summary?

Everything. Every invoice, every payment, every fee and every payout, in a read-only view under your own login. There is no private mode and nothing is hidden from the account owner.

Who is liable if something goes wrong?

You are, as the owner of the account. A chargeback, a refund or a negative balance lands on the account holder. That is the trade for a 15-year-old being able to accept a card at all, and we would rather you weigh it now than find out later.

What happens when they turn 18?

At 18 they can open and own a Stripe account in their own name, and the point is that they move onto it and you step off. We have not shipped that handover yet. When we do we will say how it works, rather than implying it already exists.
