Questions

What a shooter asks before switching.

Can I take money before the shoot and the rest after?

Yes, two ways. Publish the booking amount as its own named offer and the balance as another, or ask the buyer for a free-chosen amount. Both post to the ledger as ordinary sales.

Can I sell prints, albums or a USB?

No. The shoot, the edit and a delivered gallery are fine. A printed album, a framed piece or anything that ships in a box is not, and neither are food or crafts.

What will the charge say on my client's statement?

Fuime. Ninth Street Labs, LLC, doing business as Fuime, is the seller of record and Stripe processes the card. Worth mentioning before the shoot rather than after.

How that works.

What does a booking cost me?

5% of what you collect, with a 50¢ floor. A floor, not an additive fee: on a job you price at $400 the fee is $20.00, not $20.50.

Card processing of about 2.9% + 30¢ comes out of that 5% rather than out of you, because fuime is the legal seller and Stripe bills fuime. The full breakdown.

What if I have to refund a client?

A refund posts a reversal and rebates fuime's fee in proportion, so you are not paying a platform fee on money you gave back. A partial refund books the increment, not the running total.

When does the money for a wedding reach me?

A payout run is generated weekly, and a person approves it before anything moves: a 7-day hold, a 10% rolling reserve over 90 days, a $2,500 cap per run and a $10 floor.

Anyone skipped in a run is given a reason. There is no automated rail, and no promised date.

Do I have to be 18 to shoot for money?

No. From 13 up, a parent or legal guardian accepts a versioned agreement and becomes the account holder and legal payee, and you run the business. Signup refuses anyone under 13.

Nobody's identity is verified: the signature IP, user-agent, timestamp and document version are recorded, and that is all that is kept.
