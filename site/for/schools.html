What a school can hold

No guardian signature per student.

This is the part that usually kills a programme before week one. A school stands in loco parentis, so the per-student guardian step that a home sign-up needs does not apply to a cohort you are running.

In loco parentis, and that is enough

Because the school stands in the parent's place for the programme, a cohort does not need a signed guardian agreement for every student before the class can start.

A pooled account the school holds

One account at the institution rather than thirty separate arrangements. The school is the party on it, which is usually the only version a finance office will approve.

A treasury you fund

Put a budget behind the cohort and spend it as the term runs. What the programme has committed and what is left are both readable without an export.

Awards that settle per student

Make an award out of the treasury and it lands against the student it was given to. Grants, prizes and seed funding stop being a spreadsheet somebody maintains by hand.

How the money moves

A student sells, and the books write themselves.

A buyer pays fuime, Stripe processes the card, and fuime owes the student's venture what it earned. Nothing about the deduction is rolled up out of sight of the class.

Each venture is a real one

A name, a public storefront at /b/<business>, named offers at prices the student sets, and a payment page per offer. None of it is a simulation of selling.

The fee is a line they can see

fuime's fee posts as its own visible negative line naming the rate that was charged, which turns a lesson about margin into something a student can read instead of believe.

Receipts and comments on the line

Upload a receipt or email one in, pair it to the transaction, and thread comments on it. Marking the work means reading the record rather than trusting a summary.

The school is not the seller

Ninth Street Labs, LLC, doing business as Fuime, is the seller of record on every purchase. The liability for the sale sits with fuime, not with the institution running the class.

Questions

The ones a programme lead asks first.

Do I need a guardian signature for every student?

No. A school stands in loco parentis, so a cohort does not need a per-student guardian on the account the way a student signing up at home does. That is the single biggest difference between running this as a class and pointing thirty students at the sign-up page.

How young can a student be?

13. The age floor is hard-clamped there and under-13 is refused at signup, so a middle-school cohort has to be built around that line rather than around an exception.

What can students actually sell?

Services and digital goods, in US dollars, in the United States. Tutoring, design, video, web work, repairs, yard work. Not physical goods, food or crafts, and not childcare or babysitting. Prices are $1 to $10,000 and every student sets their own.

What does it cost the programme?

5% of what a venture collects, with a 50¢ floor — a floor, not an additive fee, so on a $400 sale it is $20.00 rather than $20.50. No monthly fee and nothing at all until something sells, so a cohort that never trades costs nothing. Unlimited businesses and API keys are included. A programme outside the standard rate is a conversation with sales, not a tier — the full breakdown.

How long before a student can sell?

Every new business is read by a person at fuime before it can take a payment. There is no service level on that review and this page will not invent one, so tell us the term dates when you get in touch rather than planning around a number.

Can students see what they owe at tax time?

They can see where they stand. fuime totals income, expenses and net for the year against the IRS $400 self-employment threshold, applying the 92.35% Schedule SE multiplier, so the real trigger is $433.14 of net profit. It is an estimator: it calculates no sales tax and files nothing — what it counts.

What happens to a venture after the course ends?

It keeps existing. The books, the storefront and the customer list belong to the venture rather than to the cohort, and a student carrying on outside the programme moves onto the ordinary arrangement, with a parent or legal guardian as the account holder — what that asks of them.

Where this stops

What fuime does not do for a cohort.

Better to read this before a term is built on it than during one.

Nobody's identity is checked

There is no identity verification of anyone in fuime. Age is a self-attested checkbox and vetting is a person reading an application. No document is collected, checked or held, of a student or of anybody else.

There is no classroom layer

No gradebook, no lesson plans, no assignment flow, no roster import. fuime is the money and the books under whatever you are already teaching, not a curriculum.

Payouts are approved by a person

Runs are generated weekly and a human approves each one. The policy is a 7-day hold, a 10% rolling reserve over 90 days, a $2,500 cap per run and a $10 floor, with a stated reason for anyone skipped. No date is promised.

USD, the US, and English

One currency, one country, one language, and no tax registration in any jurisdiction. An international campus, or a cohort selling abroad, is not something fuime can support.
