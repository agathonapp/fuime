Who this is for

Work that repeats should not need a new ask.

A weekly student. A monthly retainer. A plan a client agreed to in March and has not thought about since. The re-asking is the part that costs you the client, not the price.

The weekly student

Same hour every Tuesday, same number at the end of every month. Set it once and the charge arrives without a message that opens with an apology for bringing it up.

The retainer

A fixed amount for being available. Billing that by hand is the month you find out exactly how much you dislike invoicing, and the month a client notices you forgot.

The thing you keep running

A site you maintain, a mix you keep current, a plan you keep up to date. The work does not stop and start, so the billing should not either.

The yearly version

An offer can renew once a year instead of once a month, for a client who would rather settle the whole thing in one go and then not think about it again.

Each renewal

The charge posts itself, and so does the record.

You do not re-send anything and you do not re-enter anything. A renewal is a sale like any other sale, with the same ledger line and the same fee shown on the face of it.

It charges on schedule

On the same date each month, or each year, at the price the offer was published at. You do not send anything, and the client does not have to do anything either.

It posts to the books

The renewal becomes a ledger line as it settles, with fuime's fee on its own negative line naming the rate that was actually charged on that renewal.

It behaves like a normal transaction

Attach a receipt to it, pair one that arrived by email, or argue about it in the comment thread. Nothing about a renewal is a second-class line in the ledger.

Nothing is owed on money that never came

The fee comes out of what actually collects. A period that collects nothing is not a bill from us, and there is no monthly charge sitting underneath any of this.

What it cannot do yet

Your client cannot cancel this themselves.

There is no buyer-facing cancel button today. A client who wants out emails support@fuime.com and a person stops the subscription. Read that before you sell one, and tell the client before they buy one.

How a client stops it

They email support@fuime.com and we cancel it. There is no portal for them to log into, and no cancel link in the renewal that does it without a person reading the message.

Say it when you sell it

A client who cannot find the exit reads the next renewal as a charge nobody agreed to. That is how a conversation becomes a card dispute, and a dispute is the expensive version.

A renewal can be undone in the books

A refund posts a reversal and rebates fuime's fee in proportion, so an unwanted renewal comes off the ledger as its own event rather than being quietly edited away.

What does not exist

No buyer portal, no self-service cancel link, no reminder before a renewal, and no automatic retry sequence on a failed charge. None of that is built, so none of it is described here.

Questions

The ones worth asking before you sell one.

Can a client cancel a subscription themselves?

Not yet. They email support@fuime.com and a person cancels it. There is no buyer portal and no cancel link in the renewal, and we would rather you read that here than have a client find it out at the wrong moment.

How does a subscription stop, then?

Through support@fuime.com. Every cancellation goes through that address today, whoever asks for it, and it is handled by a person rather than a button.

Monthly or yearly?

Either. An offer bills on one cadence or the other, at the price it was published at, in US dollars. There is no weekly option and no custom interval.

Does a renewal show up in my books on its own?

Yes. Each renewal posts as its own ledger line as it settles, with fuime's fee on its own negative line naming the rate charged. You can attach a receipt and comment on it like any other transaction.

What happens if I refund a renewal?

A reversal posts to the ledger and fuime's fee is rebated in proportion. A partial refund books the increment rather than a recalculated running total, so the history stays readable afterwards.

Does recurring cost more than a one-off sale?

No. A renewal is priced exactly like any other sale: 5% of what you collect, minimum 50¢, no monthly fee, and Stripe's 2.9% + 30¢ comes out of that rather than being charged to you on top.
