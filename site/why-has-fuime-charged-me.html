Why has fuime charged me?

Because you bought something from a small business that sells through fuime. The charge reads Fuime* [business name], and fuime is there instead of the name you expected because fuime is the legal seller on that purchase.

The line on your statement

fuime, then the name of the business.

A card statement shows the legal seller, and on these purchases that is fuime. The words after the asterisk are the business you actually dealt with, put there so this is answerable.

What the charge looks like

Fuime* and then the business name, sometimes shortened by your card issuer to fit. If the second half looks like a person's initials or a trade, that is the business.

Who the seller is

Ninth Street Labs, LLC, doing business as Fuime. It is the merchant of record on the sale, which is the legal reason its name is on your statement rather than theirs.

Who ran the card

Stripe. Checkout is hosted by Stripe, so your card details went to Stripe and not to the business or to the person who did the work. Sales are in US dollars.

What you bought

A service or a digital product. Tutoring, a photo or video shoot, a lawn job, a website, a DJ set. Physical goods and food are not sold through fuime at all.

Which purchase was it

Questions

The ones people ask at this point.

I have never heard of fuime. What is it?

fuime is the software small businesses use to sell, invoice and keep books. Ninth Street Labs, LLC, doing business as Fuime, is the merchant of record on those sales, which means it is the legal seller on your purchase. That is the whole reason its name reached your statement instead of theirs.

Why isn't the name of the person I dealt with on the statement?

Because they are not the legal seller. You bought from Ninth Street Labs, LLC, and it pays the business what it has earned after fees. The name after Fuime* is the business you actually dealt with, and it is put there for exactly this moment.

How do I work out which purchase this was?

Read the words after Fuime* first, then match the date and the amount against what you booked or bought. Checkout sends a receipt by email at the time of the sale, so searching your inbox for the business name or the amount will usually turn it up.

I want a refund. Who do I ask?

Ask the business you bought from first. They know the work, and a refund on a fuime sale reverses the payment and gives back fuime's fee in proportion, so it costs them nothing to do it properly. If you cannot reach them, email support@fuime.com with the date, the amount and the name after Fuime*. We are not going to put a date on a refund here, because we do not have one to give you.

Will this charge come back next month?

Only if what you bought was a monthly or yearly plan. fuime can bill on a schedule, and a renewal charges the same card under the same name. A buyer cannot cancel a fuime subscription themselves, so email support@fuime.com to have it stopped, and tell the business too.

Did fuime add a fee to what I paid?

No. You paid the price the business set and nothing was added to your side of it. fuime takes its fee out of the business's share of the sale, and it pays the card processing out of that same fee rather than charging it to you or to them.

Who actually processed my card?

Stripe. Checkout is hosted by Stripe and the card details go to Stripe, not to the business and not to the person who did the work. Sales are in US dollars.

I do not recognise this at all.

Start with whoever else uses the card, because a tutoring session, a shoot or a yard job booked by someone in your household is the common answer. If that is not it, email support@fuime.com with the date, the amount and the name after Fuime* and we will look at the sale. If you are confident the card was used without permission, tell your card issuer as well.

Should I just dispute it with my card issuer?

That is your right and nobody here will talk you out of it. It is worth knowing that a dispute takes the money back out of a small business while it is decided, and that asking the business, or us, costs you nothing and does not tie the payment up. If you have already opened one, tell the business so they are not arguing with you in the dark.

If that did not help

Three routes, and one honest limit.

Whichever one you take, quote the date, the amount and the name after Fuime*. Those three things identify a sale on their own.

Ask the business first

They can refund the sale, and the refund gives them back fuime's fee in proportion, so there is no reason for them to stall you. They are also the only ones who know what was agreed.

If you cannot reach them

Email support@fuime.com. Give the date, the amount and the name after Fuime*, and say what you have already tried. A person reads it.

If the card was used without permission

Tell your card issuer, and tell us at support@fuime.com so the sale can be looked at from this side. Those are two different jobs and both are worth doing.

What we cannot do for you

We cannot vouch for the work, and we do not verify anyone's identity. A person at fuime reads every business's application before it can sell, and that is a review of an application, not a background check on a human being.
