What runs today,
and what doesn't.

This site once described, in the present tense, a product that did not exist yet. Later it did the reverse and told people to wait for something that had already gone live. Both are the same defect, and this page is the correction.

The first list is drawn only from what the application actually does. The second is what we know is missing, with no dates, no quarters and no order. If something is on neither list, it is safest to assume it is not there and to ask.

Running today

Twelve things you can use right now.

Every line here is something the app does, not something it is meant to do. If a claim on this site is not backed by one of these, the claim is the thing that is wrong.

Offers, payment pages and a storefront

You publish named offers at a set price between $1 and $10,000. Each one gets its own payment page and the business gets a public storefront page. Renaming an offer does not break a link you have already sent. Checkout is hosted by Stripe.

Name your own price, or let them

An offer can carry a fixed price, or it can ask the buyer to choose the amount. Sales are in US dollars.

Monthly and yearly billing

An offer can bill on a schedule, and renewals post to the ledger by themselves. A buyer cannot cancel one on their own yet, so they email support@fuime.com and a person stops it.

A real ledger, not a list of payouts

Every sale becomes a ledger line. fuime's fee posts as its own visible negative line naming the rate that was actually charged, so the deduction is on the page rather than in a footnote.

Receipts and comments on the line itself

Upload a receipt, email one in, or pair a loose receipt to the transaction it belongs to. Threaded comments hang off the ledger line, so the argument about a charge lives on the charge.

Refunds that unwind the fee too

A refund posts a reversal and rebates fuime's fee in proportion. A partial refund books the increment rather than the running total, so a sale refunded twice does not double-count.

Merchant of record

Your client buys from Ninth Street Labs, LLC, doing business as Fuime. Stripe processes the card and bills us for it out of our own fee, so card processing is never added to your side of the sale.

An income tax estimate you can read in June

fuime totals income, expenses and net for the year against the IRS $400 self-employment threshold, applying the 92.35% Schedule SE multiplier, so the real trigger is $433.14 of net profit. A tax packet can be downloaded. It estimates and it files nothing.

Guardianship, for sellers who are minors

A guardian accepts a versioned agreement and the signature IP, user-agent, timestamp and document version are recorded. They read the ledger and the payouts, are the legal payee, and can revoke at any time. A minor cannot approve their own payout.

Payout runs a person signs off

Runs are generated weekly and approved by a human before anything moves, then marked paid against a real reference. A 7-day hold, a 10% rolling reserve over 90 days, a $2,500 cap per run, a $10 floor, and a stated reason on every skip.

Schools and cohorts

A school can hold a pooled account, fund a treasury and make awards, settling per student. A school stands in loco parentis, so no per-student guardian is needed for a class to sell.

An API, included rather than sold

Three endpoints, keys shown once and encrypted at rest, 10 live keys per business and 250 live links per key. No route or body carries a business id, so a leaked key stays inside the business it belongs to. It can ask for money; it cannot move money, read the ledger, or see personal data.

Not built yet

Ten things we know are missing.

Not a queue and not a plan. Nothing here has a date, nothing here has a position, and the order is alphabetical in spirit only. Read every line as a gap, because that is what each one is.

A client cancelling their own subscription

There is no self-service cancel for a buyer. They email support@fuime.com and a person stops the billing. For a recurring product that is a real hole and we are not going to dress it up.

Sales analytics

Every sale is in the ledger and you can read it a line at a time. There is no view that turns those lines into a trend, a best-seller, a conversion rate or a chart of any kind.

Outbound webhooks

The API can create a payment link. Nothing calls your server back when that link is paid, refunded or renewed, so anything you build on top has to come and look.

Anything at all about sales tax

fuime is registered in zero jurisdictions. It calculates no sales tax, collects none, remits none and files nothing. The tax feature that does exist estimates income tax for the year and that is the whole of it.

Selling outside the United States

The product is US only and English only. There is no second country, no localised checkout and no answer for a seller or a buyer outside it.

Any currency but the dollar

Sales are USD. There is no multi-currency pricing, no conversion and no settlement in anything else, so a client who wants to pay in their own currency cannot.

Physical goods

Categories are services and digital goods. There is no shipping address, no inventory, no fulfilment and no postage anywhere in the product, so nothing that has to arrive in a box can be sold through it.

A sandbox, and client libraries

You get three endpoints and a key. There is no separate environment to try calls against without real money, and no SDK in any language, so the first call you make is a live one.

Identity verification

Nobody's identity is verified. Age is a checkbox you tick yourself. The only gate is a person at fuime reading a new business's application before it can sell, and that is a review of an application, not a check on a human being.

The handover at 18

When an operator who signed up as a minor turns 18, nothing moves the account out of their guardian's name and into theirs. The books and the client list stay put; the legal role does not transfer.

Where fuime stops on purpose

Six limits that are not gaps.

These are decisions rather than missing work, which is why they are not on the list above. None of them is waiting on anything.

fuime is not a bank and will not become one

It does not hold deposits and offers no FDIC-insured product. What you see in the app is what fuime owes you, and that is a different thing from money sitting somewhere in your name.

Nobody under 13

Signup is refused under 13 and the age floor is clamped in the code rather than asked politely in the copy. Between 13 and 17 a guardian is the legal signer, which is the part nobody else offers.

Childcare, babysitting, coaching children

Excluded on purpose, along with food and crafts. They are not categories we are waiting to add, and a business built on them will not get through the review.

fuime does not file anything

The tax feature estimates and flags a threshold. It is not advice, it is not a return, and nothing in it is sent to anyone. What gets filed is a question for someone qualified to answer it.

An API key can ask for money, never move it

A key can create and delete payment links. It cannot send a payout, read the ledger or reach personal data, and no route or body carries a business id, so a stolen key stays inside one business.

A minor never approves their own payout

The guardian is the legal payee and the approval is enforced twice, in two places, because one check is a bug away from being no check at all.

How this page stays honest

Three rules it is held to.

Nothing below the line gets a date

A quarter is a promise with the deniability built in. When something ships it moves up into the first list and the first list is the only claim we will defend.

The second list is not in an order

Nothing in it is next, ranked or queued. If you read a sequence into it you are reading something that is not there, and we would rather say that than let the layout imply it.

If one of these is what blocks you, say so

Knowing which gap is costing someone an actual sale is worth more than any amount of internal ranking. Write and tell us which line you stopped on.
