The same job, three ways

Platforms built to take the payment.

If you are shopping for software, this is the short list. Two of the three put a company between you and the card network. The third puts you there yourself, which is the part that decides most of what follows.

A merchant of record, like Paddle

The platform is the legal seller. It takes the payment in its own name, carries the dispute, and keeps a cut. Paddle is the large one: it sells into many countries and currencies and holds tax registrations in its own name, which fuime has in none.

A processor account in your own name

You are the seller, so you keep the whole relationship and all of it: the refunds, the disputes, the filings. It also needs a business in a legal name, which is the wall a seller under 18 hits first.

fuime

Also a merchant of record. A client buys from Ninth Street Labs, LLC, Stripe processes the payment, and fuime pays the venture what it earned after the fee. Smaller by every measure that can be counted: US only, USD only, services and digital work.

Why “needs to be 18” is not a law

A minor can sign a contract. It is voidable at the minor's option, which is why a processor or an account provider will not rely on one. That is a risk decision on their side, not a prohibition on yours. fuime's answer is to put a parent or legal guardian on the agreement as the legal signer and legal payee, with the young founder operating the venture day to day. Under 13, fuime refuses at signup, and the age floor is clamped at 13.

How it works today

Most of the competition is not software.

Before anyone compares platforms they compare against what they already do, which is usually a phone, somebody's personal account, and a promise.

A transfer app, through somebody else

It's her account, her name, her transaction history. You ask her for your own money. It works right until the week she forgets, or the year somebody has to explain where it all came from.

A transfer app, your own personal account

Now it is your name, which is better. It is also the same feed as splitting a dinner bill, and there is still no invoice to send and nothing to hand a client who asks for a receipt.

Cash

No record. Nothing to show a client who wants a receipt, or the IRS if you clear $400. Fast, and completely unprovable the moment anyone asks a question about it.

“I'll get you next week”

You have no invoice to point at, so following up feels like begging. It is the only option on this page that can cost you the money itself rather than just the paperwork.

Where fuime stops

The rows fuime does not win.

Every option above has a reason somebody picks it. These are the ones where fuime is the wrong answer, and no roadmap sentence changes that today.

Anywhere outside the US

USD only, English only, US only. If your clients pay in another currency, fuime is not the tool, and a platform with real international coverage is a better answer than waiting for one.

Tax, in any jurisdiction

fuime is registered in zero jurisdictions and files nothing. It estimates a young founder's own income tax against the IRS $400 self-employment threshold. That is a teaching tool, not a compliance product.

Anything you ship or hand over

Services and digital goods only. Not physical goods, not food, not crafts. Childcare, babysitting and coaching children are excluded deliberately, not by oversight.

Speed on a twenty-dollar job

A transfer app will beat fuime every time for getting $20 from somebody standing in front of you. fuime is for the client who wants an invoice and the year-end that follows it.
