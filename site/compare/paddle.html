Both are the seller of record.
The fee is a different shape.

Paddle and fuime are built on the same mechanism: the platform is the legal seller, it takes the payment in its own name, and it pays you what you earned. Paddle is far larger at it. The two places fuime differs are who it sells for and how it charges.

Go to the arithmetic

Including the sale sizes where fuime is the dearer one.

Everything stated here about Paddle comes from Paddle's own published pricing and documentation. Their rate card and their terms can change and ours cannot change them, so check the numbers against paddle.com before you decide on them.

What Paddle is better at

This is not a close comparison on scale.

fuime cannot do most of what Paddle does, in any timeframe worth putting on a page. If any of the four below is what you are buying, buy it from them.

Reach fuime does not have

Paddle publishes support for 229 countries, 33 currencies, 19 checkout locales and 17 payment methods. fuime is USD only, English only and US only, and there is no version of this page where that is a close call.

Tax registrations, in its own name

Paddle holds tax registration in more than 100 jurisdictions and files in them. fuime is registered in zero jurisdictions and files nothing anywhere. That gap is an operating budget, not a sprint, and nobody should read it as temporary.

A far larger surface to build on

Paddle's API covers roughly 25 entity types and around 60 webhook event types. fuime's is three endpoints: who am I, and list, create and delete payment links. It is included for everyone, and it is small.

Built around software businesses

Paddle's product pages are written for recurring software billing, and that is where its depth is. If you are selling seats and renewals at scale, you are their customer and not especially ours.

The arithmetic

Same headline number, different shape.

Both publish 5% and 50¢ with no monthly fee. Paddle's adds the two together. fuime's takes the larger of them, capped at the sale itself, so above $10 fuime is fifty cents cheaper and below it the floor bites.

Read the bottom row before the middle one. At $100 and above fuime charges 5% flat and Paddle's published rate adds 50¢ on top of it, which is fifty cents a sale in fuime's favour and not much of an argument. The argument that matters runs the other way: on a $5 sale fuime's floor makes the real rate 10%, and on a $1 sale it takes half. If your work is priced there, that is a reason not to use fuime, and we would rather you see it in the table than find it on an invoice.

Why the two left-hand columns say custom pricing

Paddle's pricing page says: “If you're selling products under $10 or require invoicing, contact us for custom pricing.” So those two columns are not a like-for-like comparison, and we have not guessed at the number they would quote you. If small sales are your business, ask them for it. fuime's own formula is the smaller of the sale and the larger of 5% or 50¢, so a $9 sale pays 50¢ and a $400 sale pays $20.00 rather than $20.50. Anything outside that standard rate is a conversation with us rather than a tier you upgrade into, at rushil@fuime.com. Card processing is not added on top of either number here: fuime is the legal seller, so Stripe's ~2.9% + 30¢ is billed to Ninth Street Labs, LLC and comes out of fuime's 5%.

The other difference

Who the platform is actually built for.

Paddle's depth is in recurring software billing. fuime's is in one person selling services and digital work, invoice by invoice, and in one layer nothing else in the category has.

Sellers aged 13 to 17, with a guardian

fuime is the only merchant of record that will onboard one at all. A parent or legal guardian accepts a versioned agreement and is the account holder and legal payee; the young founder operates the business. Under 13 is refused at signup.

Services and digital work, invoice by invoice

Named offers from $1 to $10,000, each with its own payment page and a public storefront, or a free-chosen amount. An offer can also bill monthly or yearly, with renewals posting on their own.

A ledger you can argue with

Every sale is a real ledger line with receipt upload, receipts emailed in, and threaded comments. fuime's fee posts as its own visible negative line naming the rate it actually charged, and a refund rebates that fee in proportion.

A person reads your business first

Someone at fuime reviews every new business by hand before it can sell, and the hold and reserve policy is written on the page rather than in fine print. Slower to start, and that is the trade.

Where fuime stops

Six reasons to pick the other one.

A comparison page that only lists wins is a brochure. These are real, current, and none of them is softened by a roadmap.

Small sales cost more here than 5%

The 50¢ floor is the price of having no monthly fee, and it lands hardest on the cheapest thing you sell. Below $10 the effective rate climbs, and at $1 it takes half the sale.

No tax registration anywhere

fuime is registered in zero jurisdictions and files nothing. The tax tracker estimates a young founder's own income tax against the IRS $400 threshold. It does not touch checkout and it does not file.

One currency, one country, one language

USD, US, English. There is no multi-currency, no localised checkout and no international payment method, so a seller with clients abroad is better served somewhere else.

Buyers cannot cancel a subscription themselves

A recurring offer renews on its own, but a buyer who wants out emails support@fuime.com and a person handles it. There is no self-service buyer portal today.

A narrow catalogue, on purpose

Services and digital goods only. No physical goods, no food, no crafts. Childcare, babysitting and coaching children are excluded deliberately and will not be approved.

Read the agreement, not the page

Every merchant of record publishes a master agreement, and that document binds where a marketing page does not. Read fuime's before you sell on it, and read any competitor's before you move.
