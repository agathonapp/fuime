What the wall actually is, and what fuime adds in its place.

The wall, stated accurately

It is a risk decision, not a prohibition.

The usual version of this is “you have to be 18”, which is not what the law says. The real shape of it is more useful, because it tells you what a fix has to look like.

A minor can sign a contract

Signing is not the problem. The problem is that the contract is voidable at the minor's option, so the other side cannot rely on it and knows that before the ink is dry.

So the account providers decline

An account in your own name, a processor account, most business services: they decline because an agreement that only binds one way is a risk they have no reason to take. Check any provider's own terms for what they actually require.

fuime's answer is an adult whose signature holds

A parent or legal guardian accepts a versioned agreement and becomes the account holder and legal payee. The young founder operates the business: writes the invoices, chases the unpaid ones, runs it. Under 13 is refused at signup.

What the “who signs” column is asking

Not who clicked, but whose signature a court would hold to. On the middle two rows the answer is nobody, which is why they work at all and also why they leave you with nothing. On fuime the answer is the parent or legal guardian who accepted the Guardian Agreement: the signature IP, user-agent, timestamp and document version are recorded, and they can revoke it at any time. There is no identity verification of anyone in fuime today, and the acceptance record is all that is kept.

What two years costs

The bill arrives later, not never.

Waiting does not pause the work. It only means the work happens with nothing written down, and the cost shows up at the far end of it.

Two years of clients you never billed

The work is real and the client is real. The payment method is a group chat and somebody's mom, and every one of those jobs leaves the same nothing behind it.

No history to show anyone

At 18 you start from zero: no books, no list of who paid you, nothing to answer “how long have you been doing this” with except a number you cannot evidence.

Nothing to point at when a client stalls

You have no invoice, so following up feels like begging. That is a skill you would be learning in the two years, and you cannot learn it without the document.

A tax year nobody can reconstruct

If you clear $400 of net self-employment income the IRS expects a return, and the honest trigger is $433.14 of net profit. Reconstructing that from a year of app transfers is somebody's April, probably a parent's.

Where fuime stops

Waiting really is simpler.

Everything fuime adds is friction of some kind. Here it is, in the order you will meet it, so the choice is made with the whole of it rather than half.

You have to ask a parent

Nothing is paid out until a parent or legal guardian has accepted the Guardian Agreement, and they are the legal payee afterwards. If that conversation is the hard part, it is genuinely the hard part.

Someone reviews you before you can sell

A person at fuime reads every new business by hand. There is no promised turnaround, because there is no queue we could quote you honestly, and waiting for a human is slower than cash.

Money does not move the moment it lands

A 7-day hold, a 10% rolling reserve over 90 days, a $2,500 cap per payout run and a $10 floor. Runs are approved by a person before anything moves, and you cannot approve your own.

It costs 5%, minimum 50¢

No monthly fee and nothing until a sale, but the fee is real and it is billed to the guardian, never to you. On a $5 sale the minimum binds and that is 10%.

Only some kinds of work

Services and digital goods, in USD, in the US. Not physical goods, food or crafts. Babysitting, childcare and coaching children are excluded deliberately.

A handover at 18 is not built yet

At 18 the books and the client list are yours to take. A formal transfer of the account-holder role has not shipped, and we would rather say so than imply it exists.
