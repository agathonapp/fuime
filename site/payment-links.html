What a link replaces

A request in a chat app is not a price.

A payment request carries your name, your phone number, and nothing at all about the work. A link carries the offer, the amount, and a checkout page that belongs to the business rather than to you personally.

It has an address

The offer lives at a fixed URL. That is something you can text, print, or read out over the phone, and nobody has to know your number to pay it.

It says what it is

A named offer at a set price, anywhere from $1 to $10,000. The client reads the name of the work and the number before a card comes anywhere near it.

It survives a rename

Change what the offer is called and the link keeps working. The flyer you printed in March still goes to the right page in September.

Somebody else handles the card

Checkout is a Stripe-hosted page. fuime never builds its own card form, and the purchase is made from Ninth Street Labs, LLC, as the seller of record.

The two addresses

One page per offer, one page for the business.

Each offer gets a page of its own. The business gets a public storefront that lists them, so a single link can carry everything you sell instead of one thing at a time.

/pay/<venture>/<offer>

The page for one thing you sell, at the price you set. This is the link you send to a client who already knows what they are buying and just needs somewhere to pay for it.

/b/<venture>

A public storefront listing what the business offers. One address for a bio, a business card, or a client who has not worked out yet which thing they want.

Renaming does not break it

Rename the offer and the old link still resolves. Anything already printed, forwarded or bookmarked keeps working, which is the difference between a link and a screenshot.

Or let them name the amount

An offer can ask the buyer to choose what they pay instead of fixing it. Useful for a tip, a top-up on a job that ran long, or work you priced in conversation.

Making one

Name it, price it, send it.

There is no page builder and no template to fill in. An offer is a name, a price, and a category, and the page exists as soon as those three do.

01

Name the offer

Whatever you would call it on the phone. Services and digital work only, so a two-hour session or a finished edit, not something that ships in a box.

02

Set the price

Anything from $1 to $10,000, in US dollars. Or leave it open and let the buyer enter the number themselves when they get to the page.

03

Send the link

Once your business has been reviewed by hand, the page takes payments. Text the link, print it, or point people at the storefront and let them pick.

Questions

The ones people ask before publishing one.

Do I need a website first?

No. The storefront at /b/<venture> is a public page you do not have to build, and a single offer page works on its own if one thing is all you sell. Plenty of businesses never send anyone anywhere else.

What happens if I rename an offer?

The link keeps working. Renaming changes what the page says, not where it lives, so a printed flyer, an old email, and a client who bookmarked it all still land in the right place.

Can the client choose what they pay?

Yes. An offer can ask for a free-chosen amount instead of a fixed price, which is how you handle a tip, a part payment, or a number you agreed on a call.

Who is the client actually buying from?

Ninth Street Labs, LLC, doing business as Fuime, is the seller of record, and Stripe processes the card. What you are owed is the sale minus the fuime fee. There is a whole page on what that means at /merchant-of-record.

What can I sell through a link?

Services and digital work, priced in US dollars. Physical goods, food and crafts are out, and so is childcare, babysitting, and coaching children. Those are deliberate exclusions, not an oversight.

What does it cost to send a link?

Nothing to publish an offer, nothing to send the link, and no monthly fee. fuime takes 5% of what you collect, minimum 50¢, and Stripe's 2.9% + 30¢ comes out of that rather than being charged to you on top.

Where it stops

Four things a payment link will not do.

Worth knowing before you build a business on one, because every limit here is a decision rather than a gap we are hoping you do not notice.

US dollars, and only US dollars

Every offer is priced and charged in USD. There is no second currency, no country selector, and no conversion happening quietly behind the checkout page.

Services and digital work only

If it ships in a box, this is the wrong tool. Physical goods, food and crafts are excluded, and so is anything involving the care or coaching of children.

No sales tax, anywhere

fuime does not calculate, collect, or file sales tax, and is registered in no jurisdiction to do it. What you owe is between you and whoever you file with.

Who is allowed to sell

Anyone in the US selling services or digital work. If you are 13 to 17, a parent or legal guardian signs once and is the legal account holder while you run the business day to day.
