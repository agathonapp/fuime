## 1. Read this first

The machine underneath this product is in better shape than the surface. The merchant-of-record checkout is built correctly — no application fee, no `stripe_account`, the fee stamped into metadata at the exact value the ledger later deducts — a buyer can never set their own price, authorization is enforced with Pundit on every Fuime controller with no cross-venture leaks, the Plaid token is encrypted and never read back, and the guardian consent record captures what it needs to. The code is also unusually honest with itself: most of the money decisions carry a comment explaining why, which is how eight reviewers were able to tell a deliberate choice from a bug.

**The single most important fact in this review is that the fix is already written and has not shipped.** Eight of the thirteen confirmed P0s — the payouts page that 500s every Wednesday, the sale that can post to the ledger twice, the second partial refund that over-debits a teen, a classmate who can stand in as a guardian, a school student who can pay themselves, a login code you can brute-force in fifteen minutes, a guardian-invite rate limit pointed at a URL that does not exist, and Terms of Service that promise 5% while you charge 7% — are fixed on the local branch and live in production right now. Landing that branch is fix number one and it is an afternoon.

For your priority — a flow a teen can finish on a phone and a parent can finish in two minutes — three things matter most. **The emails lie at the exact moment of success**: one tap on Submit sends three contradictory mails in nineteen seconds, one of them headed `[Action Needed]` and demanding a signature on an agreement that does not exist. **The parent's half of the product ends at the signature**: after signing, the guardian is dropped on the teen founder dashboard and invited to start a business of their own, is never emailed again, has no home page, and — until the change sitting uncommitted in your tree lands — has no link anywhere to the one thing only they can do, which is connect the payout destination. **The flow is long and its highest-intent moment is intercepted**: nine screens from fuime.com to a submitted application, fourteen to a published offer, and the instant a teen is admitted a full-screen blurred HCB tour overlay covers the success message and the checklist written for that moment.

For money never being wrong, three things. **Refunds and disputes are the hole**: the fee-rebate line the code posts on a refund is never settled by the sweep, so every refund leaves a teen permanently "in arrears" by Fuime's own fee, with a phantom "still coming" amount on the same page; a dispute is booked as a permanent debit and there is no handler at all for a dispute Fuime *wins*. **The weekly payout run cannot be reconciled**: batch lines record no destination and no payment reference, and nothing re-reads what is owed between Wednesday's generation and Friday's payment, so a Thursday refund is still paid out of Fuime's own cash. **The pages that state money contradict each other**: pricing says "there is no floor underneath it" when there is a 50¢ per-sale minimum, the site says "nothing is billed during the private beta" while 7% comes off every live sale, and the payouts page promises "Fuime owes you $X, paid on Friday" for an amount and a date the run will not honour.

One claim did not survive verification: two reviewers said a first-time guardian's invite link is thrown away at sign-in. It is not — `LoginsController#complete:185` carries `return_to` into the profile form, which carries it back out. That row is downgraded below.

## 5. Themes

**One branch stands between the fixes and production.** The most severe finding in this review is organisational, not technical: eight verified P0s are fixed in a working tree that has never deployed. The parallel session that wrote them is still working (the guardian-nav change is uncommitted as I write). Whatever else happens this week, the merge is the highest-value hour available. *(row 1; row 3 is the same story one commit earlier)*

**HCB's copy is still speaking through Fuime's mouth in the places with the least review.** Nobody reads `flavor_text_service.rb`, the mailer footer, the ⌘K action list, the feature-previews page or the reimbursements intake block — which is exactly why they still say "aka Hack Bank", link `hack.af` with a minor's email, offer Google Workspace, and hand out `reimburse@hcb.gg`. The pages that got attention are clean and well-commented; the leaks are all in the corners. A single lint spec over `app/` for `/bank|deposit|insured|FDIC|hack ?club|hcb|hack\.af/i` — the marketing site already has one, and it covers only `site/*.html` — would have caught most of this theme at once. *(2, 31, 32, 33, 58, 59)*

**The product tells the truth in one place and the old story in the next.** The FAQ was corrected and the Terms were not. The storefront's Public Ledger card was gated and the transparency banner above it was not. The guardian badge was carefully weakened to "Guardian on account" and the stat tile eleven lines below still says "Parent-signed". Every guardian surface branches on `merchant_of_record?` except `/guardian` itself. This is the signature of copy fixes landing in one file at a time; the durable fix is a spec per claim, not a better memory. *(14, 25, 26, 30, and the already-landed S2-1)*

**The parent is designed for and then abandoned.** The invite email, the accept page and the consent record are genuinely good work. Everything after the signature is missing: no landing page, no navigation item, no email ever again, no payout link until this week, and — on the page they do reach — no mention of the 7% or of the personal clawback liability they just accepted. The parent is the legal party in this product and has the least product built for them. *(6, 28, 30, 36, 61)*

**Money is right where it is written down and wrong where it is derived.** The checkout, the fee computation and the pending posting are careful and specced. Every defect clusters at the second step: a rebate line the sweep does not recognise, a dispute with no closing handler, a cumulative refund treated as an increment, a batch that freezes an amount and never re-reads it, a memo that mutates when it settles. Refunds and disputes are the whole of it — the one path nobody exercised end to end. *(7, 8, 41, and the already-landed M2-3)*

**Every number a user reads about money has a different source from the number they get.** Terms read a fallback constant; the learn lesson read the same one; pricing says there is no floor while a 50¢ floor applies; the payouts page states a full payable and a Friday that the hold, reserve and $10 minimum will not honour; the storefront states a 7% commission on a page that also says Fuime is the seller. Every one is a `.rb` constant away from correct, and each was wrong in a different direction. *(9, 14, 26, and the already-landed S2-1/M1-3)*

**States exist that the UI has no sentence for.** Blocked admission, rejected vetting (just fixed), suspended, revoked consent (just fixed), waived guardian, a cohort code that silently expired, a teen who turns 18, an adult who signs up. In each the state machine is correct and the screen says something written for a different state — usually "we'll review this soon", for a review that does not exist. The pattern to adopt: every terminal or blocked state owes the user a sentence naming who acts next. *(21, 37, 52, 66)*

**Mobile was designed for and then not measured.** The storefront, pay page and wizard are genuinely good at 375px. The offers list is 951px wide, the apply layout puts a screenful of prose above the first input, the agreement is a nested scroll box, the help button's only script is gated on `>= 1024px`, and the footer's legal disclosure is illegible on a phone. Each was a default that nobody viewed on a phone afterwards. *(17, 23, 34, 35, 51)*

## 6. Flow: teen and parent

Measured against `ONBOARDING_PLAN.md` §4 (target: ≤7 screens, under 5 minutes).

**Teen, fuime.com → application submitted: 9 screens, 1 email round trip, ~10 inputs.**
1. `/get-started` → 2. `/users/auth?signup=true` → 3. code screen → 4. name + 13+ → 5. home → 6. `/applications/new` → 7. business_type → 8. project_info → 9. personal_info → review → submit.
Cuts, in order of value:
1. **Delete screen 6.** `/applications/new` collects nothing from a signed-in teen — a hidden field and a static card. Create the application in `#apply` and redirect straight to `business_type`. *(−1 screen, −2 redirects)*
2. **Merge personal_info into project_info.** After #102's field cut it holds two real inputs, one of them a name signup already has. *(−1 screen)*
3. **Kill the welcome overlay for a venture with no offers** so the success flash and the checklist are what a newly admitted teen actually sees. *(−1 interception at the highest-intent moment)*
4. **Send one email, not three.** Today: "under review, 2 business days", "[Action Needed] sign the agreement", "Welcome". The rational response to the first two is to wait.
5. On every wizard step, put the question above the prose on mobile.

**Teen, submitted → published offer: +5 wizard screens, then an unbounded human vetting wait.** Skip the storefront step once a tagline exists (−1 on every offer after the first). The vetting wait is stated honestly on `/offers` and `/payouts` and nowhere else — the venture home says the opposite ("People can still pay you any amount on your storefront").

**Parent, invite email → signed: 2 emails, 4 screens.** This half works. The residual friction: they must tick "I'm 13 or older" to create their account, the agreement is a nested scroll box, and one mistyped email address loses the invite entirely.

**Parent, signed → payout destination approved: not completable.** After signing they land on the teen dashboard; the only door to `/guardian` is an avatar dropdown behind a hamburger; `/guardian` linked only Ledger and Transactions (fix uncommitted in your tree); and no email ever reaches them again. Note that `PLAID_ENV=sandbox` alongside live Stripe makes the connect step deliberately unavailable right now — an honest deferral, correctly worded on the page — so the nav fix must land *before* Plaid flips, not after.
